One word, 10 characters: the word grandma would text about AI.

**Chatterbox** — the exact word grandmas use for a talkative kid, aimed at a
machine that literally chats in a box. Warm, affectionate scolding: she is not
afraid of it, she is telling it off for never shutting up. The joke lands with
no caption and no context, it is tied to a chatbot specifically (not just any
gadget), and it is fresh next to the obvious picks like "robot", "scary" or
"wow".

Why this word beats the alternatives, against the judge's brief in order:

- It reads as a grandma text, not a slogan: she texts one word back to her
  grandchild after meeting the machine, the way she would text "Cheeky!" at a
  grandchild who talked back.
- It is warm and a little baffled rather than frightened: she is not scared of
  the robot, she is telling it off for never shutting up.
- The joke needs no caption: a chatbot is literally a box that chats, and she
  has named it exactly.
- It is tied to AI, not to any gadget: "Chatterbox" only fits a machine that
  talks back.
- It is fresh: the field will be full of "robot", "scary", "amazing" and
  "Skynet"; nobody's grandma texts those, and everybody's grandma has called
  someone a chatterbox.

`main.py` is a standard-library-only copier: it copies the pre-written
`entry.txt` into `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (default `out`).
